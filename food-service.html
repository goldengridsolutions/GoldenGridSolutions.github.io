The Problem

Energy is one of your largest
controllable costs.

Restaurants use roughly five to seven times more energy per square foot than other commercial buildings, and a high-volume quick-service restaurant can reach ten times — according to ENERGY STAR. Refrigeration, HVAC and kitchen loads run from open to close, and often through the night. Most operators treat that as fixed overhead. It isn't.

❄️
Refrigeration runs around the clock
Walk-ins, prep fridges, and display units cycle their compressors continuously — even overnight. Compressors work far harder than they need to, wasting energy every hour of every day at every location in your estate.
⚡
Electrical waste you can't see
Kitchen motors, exhaust fans, HVAC units, and compressors create electrical noise that wastes energy across every circuit in your restaurant — invisibly, continuously, at scale.
📈
The savings potential is material at any scale
Whether you operate one location or thousands, the same verified technology applies. For multi-site operators, consistent per-location savings compound into a significant portfolio-wide outcome.

Where it sits

Connected at your panel. Felt across the restaurant.

FilterPro connects in parallel at your main electrical panel. It carries no load and conditions power quality at the source, so your refrigeration, HVAC, exhaust fans and kitchen equipment all draw cleaner power.

Refrigeration & Cooling installs at the individual unit — sensor-driven control at the evaporator trims compressor runtime on specific walk-in, freezer and cooling loads for a deeper reduction.

Why it works

Proprietary technology. Real reductions.

Motors and compressors draw more power than the work they do actually requires. The difference is lost to electrical noise and reactive losses before it ever reaches the equipment — it never shows as a fault, and it appears only on the bill.

That is what FilterPro addresses — it improves the quality of the supply reaching those loads. Refrigeration & Cooling works on a completely different mechanism, on the cooling side rather than the electrical one.

How Refrigeration & Cooling actually works

The condensing unit is where 80–90% of a refrigeration system’s energy goes. Rather than throttling it, enPact watches for the moment the evaporator has done its job — then stops the compressor and lets the cold that is already in the coil do the rest.

1
Read the superheat
Two sensors track refrigerant temperature across the evaporator. Together they show the moment the evaporator reaches maximum cooling capacity.
2
Close the valve
At that point enPact interrupts the supply to the solenoid or expansion valve. The compressor stops — at peak efficiency, not at random.
3
Use what is already there
With the valve shut, the refrigerant still in the evaporator keeps cooling the space. You get the cooling without the compressor running for it.
4
Restart on demand
Once that refrigerant is spent and superheat drops to its lower threshold, the valve reopens and the compressor cycle resumes.

Note what is not in that sequence: your thermostat. Setpoints are never touched — enPact works off superheat and product temperature, so the box holds the temperature you set it to and stays HACCP-compliant. It is the compressor’s runtime that changes, not your cooling. Works with thermostatic and electronic expansion valves, alongside all major OEM equipment.

How it is measured

Every site metered. Every claim verified.

A savings figure nobody checks is a marketing number. Every enPact deployment is commissioned onto the Emissis monitoring platform, and the savings are measured against an internationally accepted methodology — IPMVP — validated for accuracy and bias. If you want to see how a savings figure was arrived at, the calculations behind it are open for you to inspect.

1
Baseline
A pre-installation survey establishes your baseline kWh and how the equipment actually performs under normal operating conditions — not nameplate ratings.
2
Forecast
Savings are modelled from your validated historical consumption, run-time analysis and equipment efficiency profiles. This is the figure the payback period is built on.
3
Monitoring
After install, consumption is collected through site meters, sub-metering or your energy management system — a like-for-like operational comparison, not a sample.
4
Verification
Actual performance is compared against baseline and normalised for outside factors such as weather and occupancy. Results are set out in a formal savings verification report.
5
Rebate
If verified savings come in below the forecast kWh, Emissis credits back the difference in capital cost needed to restore the payback period you were quoted.
