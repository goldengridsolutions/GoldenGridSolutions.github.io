Why this shows up on your P&L

This is capital equipment. Not a control layer. Not a consulting engagement.

Refrigeration is typically 40–60% of a food processing plant's electric bill — and California's industrial rates aren't coming down. Every percentage point off refrigeration compresses directly into gross margin.

But most "energy solutions" that reach your desk are one of three things: consulting, controls software, or a demand-response program. None of those are capital equipment. None depreciate. None keep saving once the vendor's monthly fee stops.

This is different. It's a patented physical device installed alongside your existing refrigeration system. Once installed, it works whether we're on the phone or not — and the savings show up whether utility rates go up or down.

Yes, this is a real device

If you've been in this industry a while, you should be skeptical. Here's what this actually is.

You've been pitched an "energy solution" that turned out to be a spreadsheet, a chart, or a curtailment program. Fair — this isn't one of those. Three things worth knowing before you hand any of this to your CFO or head of maintenance.

The Installation
It's a physical device, installed alongside your existing refrigeration system.
Nothing about your ammonia side, DX side, or controls changes. The device optimizes electrical efficiency using a proprietary magnetic electron spin technology on the electrical loads, and where applicable, adds AI-driven compressor optimization on the refrigeration control side. What you see is lower kWh on the meter feeding refrigeration — with no change in setpoints, product temperatures, food safety controls, or workflow. Your maintenance team doesn't need new training. The install is measured in hours, not days.
The Measurement
Every number we cite is read straight off a meter — not modeled, not estimated.
We use ON/OFF comparison periods: turn the device on, run for a defined window, turn it off, read the delta. That's the entire methodology. In Canada, savings held across 12 operational days on a live ammonia chiller — weather-adjusted. In the U.K., 41.5% across 8 refrigeration units at a food processing site, including two units that came in flat, because we don't hide those in the writeup. Every case study includes the full unit-level table, not just the headline number.
The Accounting
Capital equipment. Depreciated. On your books — not on a subscription.
It's booked the way any other refrigeration asset is booked — a capital purchase, on the balance sheet, depreciated over its useful life. Not a SaaS fee. Not a consulting retainer. Not a share-of-savings arrangement that erodes ROI over time. The device pays back typically in 6–12 months and keeps saving for its useful life — improving the bottom line rather than adding another operating line item. No ongoing vendor relationship required to sustain the savings.
